What to Expect at a Notary Appointment (and Why It's Easier Than You Think)

By Brenda S. Hill · July 27, 2026 · Mobile notary serving Boise & the Treasure Valley

Home › Blog › What to Expect at a Notary Appointment

If you've never had a document notarized before, the whole thing can feel a little mysterious. Is it complicated? Will it take long? Do you need to do anything special ahead of time?

Good news: a notary appointment is one of the simplest, quickest things you'll do all week — especially when the notary comes to you. Here's exactly what to expect at a notary appointment, start to finish.

Step 1: I come to you

As a mobile notary in Boise, I travel to your home, office, hospital room, coffee shop — wherever is easiest for you. No driving across town, no waiting in line at the bank, no rearranging your whole day. You tell me when and where, and I'm there.

I serve Ada, Canyon, and surrounding counties throughout the Treasure Valley, with hours most notaries don't offer: Monday through Saturday, 6am to 11pm, and Sundays from 2pm.

Step 2: I check your ID

When we sit down, the first thing I'll do is verify your identity — confirming that you are who you say you are, so the document can be trusted by whoever receives it.

Bring a current, government-issued photo ID: a driver's license, passport, or state ID. One detail that trips people up more than anything else — the name on your ID must match the name on your document. If you've recently married, divorced, or your document uses a middle initial your ID doesn't, mention it when you book so we can sort it out ahead of time instead of at the table.

Step 3: We confirm you're signing willingly and knowingly

Part of my job is simply to confirm you understand you're signing and that no one is pressuring you. I won't read your document for you or tell you what it means — that part is up to you — but I'll always make sure you're comfortable before anything gets signed.

Step 4: You sign, and I notarize

Here's the one that matters most: don't sign your document ahead of time. A notary has to witness the signature. If it's already signed when I arrive, we usually can't proceed — and that's the single most common reason an appointment has to be rescheduled.

Once you sign in front of me, I'll complete the notarial certificate, add my stamp and signature, and record the appointment in my journal. Then you get back to what matters. Most appointments take just a few minutes.

How to prepare: a short checklist

  • Have your unsigned document ready.
  • Bring a current, government-issued photo ID with a name matching the document.
  • If others need to sign (spouse, co-owner, co-borrower), make sure they're present with their own ID.
  • If there are blank spaces you're unsure about, leave them blank so we can talk through them — don't guess.
  • Know roughly how many signatures need notarizing, so I can budget the right amount of time.

How long does a notary appointment take?

A standard notarization usually takes 10 to 15 minutes from hello to handshake. A full loan or real estate signing runs closer to an hour, because there are far more pages and several of them need initials as well as signatures. Either way, I book the appropriate time slot up front so nobody feels rushed.

That's really all there is to it. Notarization exists to protect you and give your important documents credibility, and my job is to make it feel effortless. You can read what other Treasure Valley clients have said about how the process went for them.

One important note: I'm a notary, not an attorney. I can notarize your signature, but I can't give legal advice, prepare documents, or tell you which document is right for your situation — that's an attorney's job. What I can do is make the signing simple, valid, and convenient.
