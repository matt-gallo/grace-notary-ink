What Is a Mobile Notary — and Why Have One Come to You?

By Brenda S. Hill · August 31, 2026 · Mobile notary serving Boise & the Treasure Valley

Home › Blog › What Is a Mobile Notary?

Most people find out what a mobile notary is on the worst possible day — a form is due, someone can't leave the house, and the bank closed at five. So before you're in that spot, here's the short version: a mobile notary is a regular notary public who travels to you instead of making you travel to them.

Same commission, same stamp, same journal. The only difference is the address on the appointment.

What a mobile notary actually is

A notary public is commissioned by the state to witness signatures. My job is to check that you are who you say you are, confirm you're signing willingly and know what you're signing, and record it with my seal, my signature, and an entry in my journal. That's what makes a document hold up later when a bank, a court, or a county recorder looks at it.

“Mobile” just describes where that happens. I've notarized documents at kitchen tables, in hospital rooms, in the break room of a job site, in a car in a parking lot when that was the only place a client could meet. The notarization is identical either way.

Why people have a notary come to them

The obvious reason is convenience, but that's usually not the real one. The real one is almost always that going somewhere isn't practical:

  • Someone can't travel. A parent in a care facility, a spouse recovering from surgery, a family member in the hospital who needs paperwork handled today.
  • The timing doesn't work. Banks notarize during banking hours. Real life happens at 7 a.m. and 9 p.m. I work Monday through Saturday, 6 a.m. to 11 p.m., and Sundays from 2 p.m.
  • There are several signers. Getting four family members to one bank branch at one time is harder than getting me to the house.
  • The documents are sensitive. Estate paperwork and loan packages are easier to sign at your own table than at a counter in a lobby.
  • It's urgent. Deadlines don't care that it's Saturday.

You can see the full list of what I handle — from single signatures to complete loan signings and estate document packages — and the areas I cover across Boise, Meridian, Nampa, Eagle, Kuna, and Caldwell.

Your mobile notary checklist

Have these four things ready and the appointment usually takes fifteen minutes:

  • Unsigned documents. Every page, and don't sign anything ahead of time — I have to watch you sign it.
  • Valid photo ID. Unexpired, government-issued, with your photo and signature. The name on it needs to match the name on the document.
  • Every signer present. If two people sign, two people need to be there with their own ID.
  • Any required witnesses. Some documents need them and they can't be anyone named in the paperwork. Ask whoever gave you the form.

My walk-through on preparing your documents before a signing goes deeper on the small things that cause reprints.

One important limit: I can notarize your signature and make sure the signing is done correctly, but I can't tell you which document you need, fill one out for you, or explain what its terms mean. Those are legal questions and they belong to an attorney. Nothing here is legal advice.

How long does a mobile notary appointment take?

For a single document with one signer, plan on about fifteen minutes once I arrive. A power of attorney or a small estate packet runs twenty to thirty. A full real estate or refinance signing is closer to an hour, because there are a lot of pages and I'd rather you understand where you're signing than rush you through it.

Most of the delay in a notary appointment isn't the notarizing — it's a missing ID, a missing signer, or a page that was already signed. Handle those in advance and it's quick.
