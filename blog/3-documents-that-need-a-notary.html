3 Documents People Are Surprised to Learn Need a Notary

By Brenda S. Hill · July 31, 2026 · Mobile notary serving Boise & the Treasure Valley

Home › Blog › 3 Documents You Didn't Know Need a Notary

Most people know a notary handles the "big" paperwork — buying a house, refinancing a loan. But notarization shows up in a lot of everyday moments too, and those are the ones that catch people off guard.

Here are three documents that need to be notarized more often than people expect, and what usually goes wrong with each.

1. A letter allowing your child to travel without you

Sending your teenager on a trip with grandparents, a coach, or a family friend? Many airlines, border crossings, and program organizers ask for a notarized parental consent letter — sometimes called a travel authorization — especially for international travel or when only one parent is signing.

This is the one I get called about at the worst possible moment: the night before a flight. Requirements vary by airline and destination, so check with whoever is asking for it, then handle the notarization a week or two ahead. It takes minutes when there's no clock running.

2. A title transfer for a vehicle, boat, or trailer

Selling or gifting a car, RV, boat, or trailer? In Idaho, the title transfer or bill of sale often needs to be notarized before the new owner can register it.

People usually learn this at the DMV counter — after they've already made the trip. Worse, the buyer and seller have often gone their separate ways by then, and getting both signatures together again is a headache. Notarizing at the handoff keeps it clean.

3. A medical or financial authorization for someone you trust

Maybe you want a family member to speak with your doctors, or to handle an account if you can't. These documents frequently need to be notarized to be honored.

Families often set these up before a surgery, a deployment, or a parent's move into assisted living — so they're ready before they're needed. I visit hospitals and care facilities regularly for exactly this reason, and I'd much rather help someone plan ahead than scramble.

Honorable mentions

  • Affidavits and sworn statements
  • Permission and consent forms
  • Certified true copies
  • Real estate and loan documents — the classics (see all services)

How do I know if my document needs a notary?

The simplest rule: look for the signature block. If your form has a line that says "Notary Public," "sworn and subscribed before me," or a blank space marked for a seal or stamp, that's your cue.

If there's no such line but the person receiving the document asked for it "notarized," ask them what exactly they need. Sometimes they want a notarized signature; sometimes they want a certified copy. Those are different things, and knowing which saves a second appointment.

What to have ready

  • The unsigned document — don't sign until we're together
  • A current, government-issued photo ID with a name matching the document
  • Everyone who needs to sign, present at the same time, each with their own ID
  • Any instructions from whoever requested the notarization

Not sure whether yours qualifies? Just ask — I'm happy to point you in the right direction, and it costs nothing to check. You can also see how the process has gone for other Treasure Valley families.
