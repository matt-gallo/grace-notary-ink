The first time I set a loan package on someone's kitchen table, they looked at the stack and asked if I'd brought the wrong file. A hundred and forty pages. For one house.

Nobody warns you about the size of it, so this week I'm warning you — and then taking the fear out of it. A real estate or refinance signing is the most paper you'll ever sign in one sitting, but it's also one of the most predictable appointments I do. The package looks the same from one closing to the next.

The short version:

✓Plan on about an hour. What stretches it isn't the page count — it's a missing ID or a name that doesn't match.
✓The Note gets signed. The Deed of Trust gets notarized. It's the longest thing in the stack.
✓Keep your loan officer's number within reach. If a number on the page doesn't match what you were told, we pause and you call. That's the right move every time.
✓Sign nothing ahead of time. Not one page.

One thing I'll say plainly: I can tell you what each document is called and where to sign it. I can't tell you what the terms mean or whether the numbers are right — that's for your loan officer, your title officer, or an attorney.
